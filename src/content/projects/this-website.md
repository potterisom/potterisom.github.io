## Who it’s for

People land on a personal site for a few seconds and decide what they think. So the goal wasn’t to list everything I’ve done; it was to show product taste.

## How I got to the fog

I borrowed Felix Rieseberg’s process: dozens of rough sketches, ruthless notes, and riffing only on what survived. The first eight directions were all derivative. The next rounds were themed — tea, cards, chips — and generic underneath.

Then I asked a different question: what is this website, physically? Twelve answers later, one sketch — fogged glass you wipe with your cursor — got a note in all caps. The final homepage is just that, over one photograph.

> “slick and simple and cool”

## How it’s built

The fog is a canvas you erase with the cursor; it slowly grows back. Every sound is generated in the browser — bubbles, not static. The site is static and hosted on GitHub Pages, so adding an essay or a project is one file.
